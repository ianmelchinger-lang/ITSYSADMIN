Put the Moves in an Order That Actually Works

A working file on the storefront host needs to end up at its documented home, with something to show a colleague that it landed correctly. Here are the six moves that get there, out of order. Arrange them into a sequence that could actually be carried out — some orders simply can't be, no matter how you argue for them.

Use the arrows to reorder the list, then check it. A move is out of place when it depends on something that hasn't happened yet in your sequence — that's what gets flagged, not a single fixed answer.

    None of these six moves is hard on its own. What makes the sequence real is that each one supplies something the next one needs — a destination you can't check against a target you never stated, a record of a change you never verified. Get the order right and a colleague can follow it without asking you anything. Get it wrong and the sequence breaks somewhere it's easy not to notice.
