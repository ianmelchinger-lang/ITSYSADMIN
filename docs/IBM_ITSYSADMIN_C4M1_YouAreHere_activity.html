Who Can Still Sign In?

At Sample Enterprise Manufacturing, the plant floor runs its own terminal system, separate from the corporate office network. An operator on the production line leaves the company. A routine request comes in: remove their access.

IT support disables the operator's account on the corporate network and closes the request. Nothing else is flagged. From where support sits, the case looks closed.

Before you see what happens next — has this operator's access actually ended?

The production-floor terminal the operator used to clock in and log output runs on a different system, with its own separate account record. Disabling the operator's account on the corporate network has no effect on that record — nothing connects the two systems.

The operator's login on the plant-floor terminal was never touched. Weeks after the request was closed, it still opens the terminal exactly as before.

Closing the request recorded that someone acted. It did not record whether every system holding this operator's account had actually been checked.

Removing someone's access means finding and clearing their account everywhere it exists — not only in the one system that came to mind first.
