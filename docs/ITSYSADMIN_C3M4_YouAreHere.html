The Check That Left No Record

A routine you do every day, and a question you can't answer.

The situation

You've taken over the Windows back office for a mid-market retailer's IT function. Three people work in it. A morning check comes with the job:

  • Open a console on the server.
  • Confirm a handful of services are running.
  • Restart any that aren't.
  • Check how much storage is left.
  • Confirm last night's checks completed.

It takes about forty minutes, every day. Of the three of you, only you can do it.

On Friday, the operations lead asks whether the check ran on Tuesday. The console keeps no history of what you clicked.

What exists to show the check ran this week
  • MonNo record
  • TueNo record. This is the day you're asked about.
  • WedNo record
  • ThuNo record
  • FriNo record
Which fix do you reach for first?

Pick the one you'd actually start with. You'll lock it in before seeing what it leaves out.

What your answer leaves out

Three questions a console never asked you

  • When a step says "restart," which services will it restart? Can you tell before it runs?
  • Will this server agree to run the file at all? If it refuses, what is the refusal telling you?
  • Could a colleague run the file tomorrow without asking you what it's for?

Narrow the set before you act on it

The capability behind all three questions is deciding exactly what a step applies to before deciding what the step does. It has to be in place before a check can be handed over, scheduled, or run on more than one server.
