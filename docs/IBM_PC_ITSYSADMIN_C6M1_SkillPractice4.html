Evaluate the Log Review Process

A log informs someone only when a person reads it against an expectation and passes on what they find. Switching logging on is a setting. A review is an act that recurs, so a written procedure has to say enough for someone to check that the act happened.

What a review needs

NIST's security control on reviewing audit records, AU-6, asks for review at a defined frequency, for signs of unusual activity, with findings reported to named people or roles. In plain terms, a review has three parts.

  • A frequencyHow often someone reads the log.
  • An expectationWhat the log should contain.
  • A recipientThe named person who hears what was found.

A sentence fails the test if it leaves one of these vague, or if it describes something other than review, such as a setting or a response.
