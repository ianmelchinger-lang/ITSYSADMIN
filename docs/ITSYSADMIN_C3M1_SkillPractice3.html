The share, or its access list?

A share is a folder published on the network under a name, so other machines can reach it. Its access list is a separate thing: the record of who is allowed to open it. Publishing the folder and naming who may reach it are two different acts, and the second is where restraint lives.

That is why hardening guidance can call an over-broad share a finding without knowing anything about the files inside it. The finding is about the list, not the contents.
